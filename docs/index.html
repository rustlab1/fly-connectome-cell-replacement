How to read the model

Neurons, connections and movement

The model uses 165,122 neurons from the male fly CNS connectome. Visual inputs generate spikes; motor responses determine turning and forward movement in a simplified steering controller.

Site 20 affects a descending motor-control interface. It is not a fly equivalent of motor cortex. Its 1,000-cell lesion removes 37 descending and four lobula plate tangential neurons.

What the uncertainty means

Each result uses 100 neural and intervention realizations within one reconstructed nervous system. Movement is averaged over 20 matched gust sequences before calculating uncertainty. These are model realizations, not 100 animals.

Lesion bands show the central 95% simulation range, matching Figure 1f. Replacement bands show pointwise 95% confidence intervals for the mean, matching Figure 2c. The animation shows one prespecified example, not the mean path.

What replacement means here

Original repair is an ideal reconstruction of selected lost cells and their available partners. Full original repair reproduces intact by construction. A graft with the correct types instead inherits connections from surviving cells of those types.

At full dose, 42 untyped positions in the correct-type condition receive sampled lost-cell identities. Replacement is immediate in the model; graft maturation and spontaneous recovery are not simulated.

Interpreting the readouts

Intact function is one. Values above one can occur and are retained. Wing-motor and looming readouts measure neural activity, not physical wing posture or observed takeoff. Virtual crossings describe flight planes, not walking beam counts.

The controller retains a minimum forward speed, so severe lesions can leave small residual displacement even at zero neural motor drive.

Source data

The controls use the final results of the preprint and tested 0–2,000-cell replacement series. Firing traces recover the same first neural realization and are verified against its saved rates.
