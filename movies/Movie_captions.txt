Supplementary Movie 1. Lesion size and location. The first ten seconds compare 1,000-, 4,000- and 16,000-cell lesions at site 20 (top row) and site 25 (bottom row), with six matched 3D paths. The next 18 seconds reveal nine nested site-20 lesions, from 250 to 64,000 lost neurons, and all five Figure 1f readouts. These are separate lesion conditions, not progressive injury over time. Points show means and ribbons the empirical central 95% simulation range across 100 realizations, with 20 gusts averaged for movement. Spared visual activity has a separate panel. The anatomy marks actual lesion masks.

Supplementary Movie 2. Neuronal responses to looming input, comparing intact, a 2,000-cell site-20 lesion and 100 replacements with original connections. Nine seconds of playback show 59–300 ms of the 300-ms trial at 30 fps. Purple marks input, blue host, teal replacement and orange motor activity, with an 8-ms display decay. Motor traces show the arithmetic mean of TTMn and DLMn population rates over the preceding 25 ms, matching the looming motor readout in the figures. Anatomy and rate axes share scale.

Supplementary Movie 3. Selecting cells to restore after a 2,000-cell site-20 lesion. Four matched ten-second paths compare no graft and 100 lost cells selected randomly, by projection priority or by motor-control priority. All replacements retain their original identities and available partners. Random selection chooses missing cells and differs from random wiring in Figure 2. Colored anatomical points in the insets show the selected cells within the shared lesion. This comparison corresponds to Supplementary Figure 4a.

Supplementary Movie 4. Replacement identity and wiring. Five Figure 2 conditions are compared: no graft, original repair, random wiring, correct-type graft and non-target graft. Separate ten-second chapters show 100 and 2,000 intended replacements after the same 2,000-cell lesion, matching Figure 2b. These are separate doses, not graft growth or maturation. Correct-type and non-target grafts copy partners from surviving cells of the assigned type using the same integration rule. Tables show mean distance, wing-motor and looming-motor output across 100 realizations, normalized to matched intact, rather than the single example paths. Only complete original repair reproduces intact exactly; random wiring and non-target identity remain impaired at full dose. All panels share one camera and scale.

All examples use the prespecified neural seed 1000 and intervention seed 2000, with gust seed 3 for movement. Movies 1, 3 and 4 replay saved horizontal paths at 60 fps from one fixed camera above a 0.5-m grid, with positions interpolated from the saved paths without smoothing. Grey dashed paths show matched intact and open circles mark each second; in the anatomical insets, pink marks lesions. Each panel also gives the mean distance across 100 realizations. The fly is enlarged; its altitude, posture and wingbeat are illustrative. These films demonstrate simulated results. Historical and superseded movies are archived outside the current paper package.
